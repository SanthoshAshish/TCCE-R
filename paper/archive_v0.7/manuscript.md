## Abstract

**Purpose.** Self-evolving communication networks can rewire links to keep distributed consensus working under failures. We ask whether a resilience-aware design, TCCE-R, which combines a consensus-error signal with graph-structural fragility (bridge fraction) and link-state volatility, accumulates them into a "resilience debt", and adapts its trigger using counterfactual feedback, improves consensus over simpler rewiring policies.

**Methods.** We evaluated eight policies in a simulator under one frozen, pre-registered protocol: three synthetic scenarios (random geometric graphs with 40 or 80 nodes; transient, background and spatially correlated burst failures) and two rewiring budgets (M = 3 and 6). Mutation thresholds were calibrated on separate seeds using mutation counts only, frozen with a SHA-256 manifest, and evaluated once on 60 held-out seeds per condition (2,640 of 2,700 planned runs; one arm in one condition could not be calibrated). Arms were compared paired by seed with Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, Holm correction, bootstrap confidence intervals, and a practical-effect margin of 0.02 in consensus error.

**Results.** Full TCCE-R (A5) was not shown to be better than the simple reactive threshold (A1): in four of the five evaluable conditions the two were practically equivalent, and in one (S2, M = 3) A1 had slightly lower error (ΔE = +0.0105, below the margin). A state-blind front-loaded schedule (A7) had lower consensus error than A5 in all six conditions, by 0.024 to 0.076 in mean paired difference (Holm p ≤ 0.0031; all point estimates exceed the 0.02 margin, though the CI lower bound is just below it in two conditions). Within the accumulated-debt family, adding connectivity and volatility signals without adaptation (A4 vs A3) did not help, and adaptive thresholds with counterfactual feedback (A5 vs A4) made no detectable difference. These results are specific to the simulated conditions and do not isolate the individual resilience signals, which were not ablated.

**Conclusion.** Under this protocol, resilience-aware control logic did not outperform simpler alternatives, and the timing of the first rewirings appears to matter more than the trigger's sophistication. We report this as a bounded negative result together with the protocol, code and diagnostics needed to audit it.

**Keywords:** distributed consensus; self-evolving networks; topology adaptation; network resilience; algebraic connectivity; negative results

## 1 Introduction

Distributed averaging and consensus protocols let nodes in a network agree on a common value using only local exchanges, and are a building block of sensor fusion, coordination and distributed estimation (Olfati-Saber et al. 2007; Xiao and Boyd 2004). Their convergence speed is governed by the connectivity of the communication graph, in particular its algebraic connectivity λ₂ (Fiedler 1973; Olfati-Saber and Murray 2004). When links fail, are repaired, or are lost in bursts, the effective graph changes and consensus degrades (Kar and Moura 2009). One response is to let the topology itself adapt, an idea studied under adaptive coevolutionary networks (Gross and Blasius 2008), topology control (Santi 2005), virtual-topology adaptation (Gençata and Mukherjee 2003) and edge rewiring for robustness or algebraic connectivity (Chan and Akoglu 2016; Sydney et al. 2013; Ghosh and Boyd 2006).

A natural design intuition is that a controller aware of *resilience* (how fragile the graph is, how volatile its links are, how much consensus-error stress has accumulated) should decide *when* and *where* to rewire better than one reacting to an instantaneous error. TCCE-R (Topology–Consensus Co-Evolution with Resilience debt) is a nine-stage architecture built on this intuition. This paper asks whether the intuition survives a controlled test. We do not present TCCE-R as a proven improvement; the central finding is that, under the frozen evaluation, it does not establish consistent superiority over a simple reactive baseline, and that a state-blind front-loaded schedule achieved lower consensus error than full TCCE-R in every condition.

**Research questions.**
- **RQ1.** Does full TCCE-R (A5) reduce consensus error relative to a reactive threshold policy (A1)?
- **RQ2.** Does front-loaded rewiring (A7) differ from adaptive TCCE-R (A5)?
- **RQ3.** Do the connectivity (C) and volatility (V) signals add value beyond a consensus-error-only accumulated debt (A3 → A4), and does adaptive thresholding with counterfactual feedback add value beyond that (A4 → A5)?
- **RQ4.** What trade-offs appear among consensus error, connectivity, recovery time and reconfiguration activity?
- **RQ5.** What do the results establish, and not establish, about resilience-aware control logic?

**Contributions.** (i) A fully specified, reproducible simulation protocol with calibration on separate seeds, frozen configuration hashes and a single held-out evaluation. (ii) A matched-budget comparison of eight timing policies sharing one candidate selector, safety gate and edge budget, so that only *when* to mutate differs. (iii) An honest report that the proposed full method is not superior, with mechanism-oriented exploratory diagnostics and an analysis of why a legacy before/after feedback rule is biased by regression to the mean. We make no claim of novelty beyond this combination, and no claim that the individual signals are ineffective.

## 2 Background and Related Work

**Consensus and connectivity.** In average consensus each node repeatedly replaces its value by a weighted combination of its neighbours' values; the weight matrix determines the asymptotic rate (Xiao and Boyd 2004), and for fixed weights the rate is tied to the second-smallest Laplacian eigenvalue λ₂, the algebraic connectivity (Fiedler 1973). Switching topologies and delays have been analysed in (Olfati-Saber and Murray 2004), and nearest-neighbour coordination in (Jadbabaie et al. 2003). Random link failures and noise were treated in (Kar and Moura 2008; Kar and Moura 2009), which also address topology design under random links. Robust average-consensus sensor fusion is considered in (Xiao et al. 2005), and resilience to misbehaving nodes in (LeBlanc et al. 2013). Our setting differs: failures are benign (outages, bursts), nodes are honest, and the controller changes the topology.

**Topology adaptation and rewiring.** Edge rewiring to improve robustness or algebraic connectivity is studied in (Chan and Akoglu 2016; Sydney et al. 2013), and greedy or convex methods for growing well-connected graphs in (Ghosh and Boyd 2006); our selector uses the first-order Fiedler-value gain estimate from the last reference. Connectivity control for mobile robot networks is surveyed in (Zavlanos et al. 2011), and topology control for wireless ad hoc networks in (Santi 2005). Virtual-topology adaptation under dynamic traffic (Gençata and Mukherjee 2003; Gençata 2003) motivates our idea that a controller may reconfigure when a signal accumulates. Adaptive coevolutionary networks, in which dynamics and topology influence each other, are reviewed in (Gross and Blasius 2008); self-healing networks in (Quattrociocchi et al. 2014). Resilience as a discipline, and vulnerability to spatially correlated disasters (which motivates our burst model), are discussed in (Sterbenz et al. 2010; Neumayer et al. 2011).

**Event- and change-triggered control.** Triggering policies that act only when a signal warrants it are established in control (Tabuada 2007; Dimarogonas et al. 2012). Our accumulated debt is Page's one-sided CUSUM (Page 1954) applied to a stress signal.

**Statistical methodology.** Paired nonparametric tests (Wilcoxon 1945), Holm's sequentially rejective correction (Holm 1979), the bootstrap (Efron 1979) and equivalence-style reasoning with a smallest effect size of interest (Lakens 2017) underlie the analysis. Regression to the mean, a pitfall for before/after evaluations triggered by extreme values, is reviewed in (Barnett 2005).

**Position.** We know of no prior controlled comparison of these specific timing policies under a common selector and budget; this statement reflects a limited, non-systematic literature review (`docs/LITERATURE_REVIEW.md`) and is not a novelty claim.

## 3 System Model and Problem Formulation

**Network and failures.** A network has *n* nodes in the unit square (n = 40 or 80) and a set of *candidate* links between node pairs within radio range. A subset is *configured* (initially links within an initial radius). Time is divided into epochs k = 0,…,79, each consisting of T = 60 consensus rounds. In each epoch a configured link is *live* if it is not failed and not in setup. Three failure processes act: (a) transient outages, each configured link being unusable in each round independently with probability 0.05; (b) background on/off failures per epoch with fail probability p_fail and repair probability 0.1; (c) spatially correlated bursts: in S1 and S3, a burst starts with probability 0.1 per epoch, fails every potential link whose midpoint lies within radius 0.15 of a uniformly random centre for 2–6 epochs. S2 has no bursts and a higher background failure rate (Table 2). A new link becomes usable only after a setup delay of 2 epochs; a removed link disappears immediately.

**Consensus error.** At the start of every epoch each node draws fresh initial values x(0). Over T rounds nodes apply Metropolis-weight averaging on the live graph of that round: for live neighbours *i, j*, W_ij = 1/(1 + max(d_i, d_j)), where d_i is the live degree, and W_ii = 1 − Σ_j W_ij. With x̄ the mean of x(0), the normalised error is

$$e(t)=\frac{\lVert x(t)-\bar x\mathbf 1\rVert}{\lVert x(0)-\bar x\mathbf 1\rVert},\qquad E_k = 1-\min\Bigl(1,\max\Bigl(0,\frac{\ln e_k(T)}{\ln\varepsilon}\Bigr)\Bigr),\quad \varepsilon=0.01 .$$

E_k ∈ [0, 1]: 0 means the error fell to ε or below within the epoch, 1 means no progress. **Lower E is better.** The primary outcome of a run is the mean of E_k over its epochs.

**Control problem.** A logically centralised controller observes the graph and the history of E_k, and may *mutate* the topology at most once per epoch and at most M times per run (budget). Every mutation is a *rewire*: it removes one configured link and adds one candidate link (strict edge budget). The question is *when* to attempt a mutation, with candidate selection and safety checks held identical across arms.

**Scope of the implemented model.** The controller is logically centralised (global graph information); we do not study distributed control. Node load, traffic conditions, trust/uncertainty and adversarial attacks, which appear in the original architecture sketch, are **not implemented**. R (resilience) is operationalised provisionally as λ₂ of the unweighted live-graph Laplacian; the term "resilience cost" in the original diagram is undefined, so M below is a ranking, not a benefit/cost ratio.

## 4 TCCE-R Architecture and Design

Figure 1 shows the nine stages: (1) Dynamic Network State, (2) Distributed Consensus Monitor, (3) Resilience-Debt Estimator, (4) Adaptive Mutation Trigger, (5) Consensus–Resilience Coupling Evaluator, (6) Constraint/Safety Gate, (7) Topology Mutation Selector, (8) Reconfigured Network, (9) Feedback Loop.

![Figure 1. TCCE-R architecture as implemented. Blue stages implement the proposed mechanism or differ between compared strategies; grey stages are the shared environment. The controller is logically centralised. Not implemented as mechanisms: load, traffic, trust/uncertainty, attack model; R = λ₂ is provisional.](figures/fig1_architecture.png)

**Signals.** Let L be the set of live links of the epoch's graph and Q the set of configured links.

- Consensus error E_k (Section 3).
- Structural fragility: $C_k = 1$ if the live graph is disconnected, otherwise $C_k = \#\text{bridges}/|L|$.
- Link volatility: $V_k=\min\bigl(1,\ \tfrac{1}{|Q|}\sum_{w=k-2}^{k}\text{flips}_w\bigr)$, where flips counts changes of up/down state of configured links over the last 3 epochs.

**Stress and debt.** The stress is $s_k = w_E E_k + w_C C_k + w_V V_k$. The resilience debt is Page's one-sided CUSUM,

$$D_k=\max\bigl(0,\ D_{k-1}+s_k-\kappa\bigr),$$

reset to 0 only after a successful mutation. In the evaluated arms, A3 uses s = E with reference κ_E; A4 and A5 use s = 0.5E + 0.3C + 0.2V with reference κ_s (weights fixed in code; no weight tuning was performed). κ_E and κ_s are the medians of E and of the stress over connected epochs of the static arm A0 on calibration seeds (Table 2).

**Trigger.** A mutation is attempted if D_k > Θ and at least 2 epochs have passed since the last successful mutation. In A5, Θ is adaptive within [Θ₀/4, 4Θ₀]: Θ ← 0.9Θ after a success, Θ ← 1.25Θ after a failure, unchanged if neutral.

**Candidate ranking M.** Candidate rewires are ordered lexicographically by (i) change in number of connected components, (ii) a first-order estimate of the gain in λ₂ for the added link, $(f_i-f_j)^2$ with f the Fiedler vector (Ghosh and Boyd 2006), (iii) change in bridge count, (iv) link length. The top 10 form a shortlist; gains within 10% of the best are treated as ties. This is a ranking, not a ratio.

**Safety gate.** A candidate is accepted only if all hold, in order: no touched edge is in its cooldown (5 epochs); the added link is not currently failed; the edge budget is respected (every mutation is a pure addition-plus-removal); both endpoints keep degree ≤ 10 (configured basis); the number of components does not increase (checked on the final and the interim setup state); and λ₂(after) ≥ min(R_min, λ₂(before)) with R_min = 0.05. The selector tries candidates in ranked order (at most 30) and applies the first accepted one.

**Counterfactual feedback (A5).** For a mutation at epoch m, the controller replays the window {m+1, …, m+d+H} (d = 2 setup epochs, H = 3 horizon) with and without the mutation under the identical observed failures, outages and initial values x(0). With Δ the mean of E(with) minus the mean of E(without), the outcome is *success* if Δ < 0, *failure* if Δ > 0, *neutral* if Δ = 0. Each mutation is evaluated in isolation and no future information is used. This replaces an earlier before/after rule that is biased by regression to the mean (Section 8.3). The replay assumes an exact model of the consensus dynamics.

**Calibration.** Thresholds (h for A1/A2, Θ for A3/A4, Θ₀ for A5) were chosen on calibration seeds 200–219 over a 40-point grid (h: 0.30–0.98 linear; Θ and Θ₀: 0.05–20 geometric) by picking, per condition, the value whose *mean uncapped mutation count* was closest to M. A threshold was feasible only within ±15% of M. No outcome metric (E, connectivity, recovery) was used. A6 and A7 draw their per-seed intended mutation counts from the empirical distribution of capped A1–A5 realised counts on calibration seeds. A1 in S3 with M = 3 was not calibratable (best mean count 2.50, below the window 2.55–3.45); its 60 runs were excluded, giving 2,640 of 2,700 planned runs, and every comparison involving it is omitted. All other arm–condition cells were feasible (Table A2). The calibration comprised 12,640 runs (the protocol planned 12,660; the 20-run difference equals the capped count-distribution runs of the one non-calibratable A1 cell). Configurations, constants and calibration report (47 files) were frozen with a SHA-256 manifest and committed (commit 7a81352, which is two commits after the repository tag `v0.7` = f874ad7, the protocol commit) before evaluation.

**Evaluation.** Held-out seeds 7000–7059 (60 per condition) were run once from a clean tracked tree at commit 7a81352 (Python 3.11.9, numpy 2.3.4, scipy 1.17.1, pandas 2.3.3; 272.3 s). Development seeds 300–319 were used only for tests and smoke runs.

**Statistics.** Within each condition arms are paired by seed (n = 60). For a comparison X − Y the difference is ΔE = E_X − E_Y; negative favours X. We use two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (p = 1 if all differences are zero), Holm correction within the primary family (12 pre-specified comparisons, 11 evaluable) and the secondary family (42 pre-specified, 38 evaluable), 95% bootstrap CIs (10,000 resamples, seed 20261010), and rank-biserial effect sizes (in the repository outputs). The practical-effect margin is |ΔE| = 0.02. A verdict is *meaningfully better* (Holm p < 0.05 and |ΔE| ≥ 0.02), *better below margin* (Holm p < 0.05, |ΔE| < 0.02), *practically equivalent* (Holm p ≥ 0.05 and 95% bootstrap CI within ±0.02) or *inconclusive*. Conditions are never pooled. **Primary comparisons:** A5 − A1 and A5 − A7. **Secondary:** A2 − A1, A3 − A1, A3 − A2, A4 − A3, A5 − A4, A6 − A1, A7 − A1. Everything else (connectivity, λ₂, recovery, activity, mechanism, placebo diagnostics) is **exploratory** and carries no p-values.

**Exploratory metrics.** Disconnected-epoch fraction; mean λ₂; anchored recovery time: for each epoch s at which a disconnection episode of the static arm A0 (same seed) begins, the number of epochs t − s until the arm's own live graph is first connected at some epoch t ≥ s (right-censored at the end of the run, counted as 80 − s), averaged over that run's anchors (`experiments/analyze_v06.py`, function computing `anchored_recovery`); runs without anchors are missing. The threshold τ in Table 2 is a 75th-percentile of A0's E used for episode labelling (`tau_quantile` = 0.75); realised mutations; and *live-link releases* (working links removed by rewiring).

**RQ1 (A5 vs A1).** A5 − A1 was practically equivalent in four of five evaluable conditions (ΔE from −0.0053 to +0.0080); in S2 with M = 3, A1 had lower error by 0.0105 (Holm p = 0.033), below the practical margin. We therefore find **no evidence that A5 improves on A1**, and no evidence that it is worse by a practically meaningful amount; the A1 S3 M = 3 condition could not be evaluated.

**RQ2 (A5 vs A7).** A7 had lower E than A5 in all six conditions (ΔE = +0.0244 to +0.0756), every mean difference exceeding the 0.02 margin with Holm p ≤ 0.0031. In S2 M = 6 and S3 M = 3 the 95% CI lower bounds (+0.0184 and +0.0188) fall slightly below the margin, so the *direction* of A7's advantage is clear in all six conditions but its *size relative to the margin* is less certain in those two. This does not mean A7 is universally superior: it is a state-blind schedule evaluated only in these simulated conditions, and it has costs (Section 6.4).

**Mechanism (exploratory).** Table A3 shows that reactive and CUSUM-type arms mutate overwhelmingly *while the network is disconnected* (A1: 45–99%; A5: 47–98%), whereas A7 does so for 7–30%, and its first mutation is at epoch 0. A reconnecting move repairs a failure that may heal anyway and may be disproportionately wasted in bursts; an early move improves the baseline connectivity (λ₂) for the whole run. We offer this as a hypothesis consistent with the data, not as a tested causal result.

## 7 Discussion

**What the evidence supports.** Under this frozen protocol (i) full TCCE-R is not shown to beat the simple reactive threshold, and (ii) front-loaded timing beats TCCE-R in every condition. A sensible reading is that, for these scenarios and budgets, *when* the first rewirings happen (early, before failures accumulate) mattered more than the sophistication of the trigger. The accumulating-debt and adaptive-feedback machinery added complexity without a detectable return.

**What it does not support.** We did not ablate C, V or D individually from A5, so we cannot say these signals are ineffective; A4 vs A3 compares *adding both* C and V to the stress with fixed weights (0.5/0.3/0.2) and a fixed Θ. We cannot claim A7 is generally superior: it is state-blind, has costs in live-link releases and, in S2, in disconnection and recovery. Results are for three synthetic scenarios, two budgets and one controller design; they should not be generalised to real networks, other graph families or distributed controllers. No pooled winner is declared.

**Why might resilience-aware triggering not help?** Hypotheses, none tested here: the debt reacts to degradation after it has begun, whereas rewirings give most benefit when applied early; mutations triggered while disconnected may be spent repairing transient failures; and the counterfactual feedback optimises a short local window rather than long-run error. The mechanism table (A3) is consistent with the first two.

**Statistical vs practical significance.** Several A5 − A7 differences are strongly significant and large relative to the margin; the single significant A5 − A1 result is smaller than the margin. We deliberately report CIs with the margin rather than p-values alone.

## 8 Threats to Validity

1. **Construct validity.** E is a consensus-error proxy normalised per epoch with fresh initial values; R = λ₂ is provisional; M is a lexicographic ranking rather than a benefit/cost measure.
2. **Internal validity.** Arms are matched on mean, not per-seed, mutation counts (realised counts reported; equal-count analysis exploratory). A5 differs from A1 in several ways at once (debt accumulation, C/V terms, adaptive Θ, feedback), so differences cannot be attributed to a single factor. A5's weights are hard-coded in the policy (no ablation). The counterfactual replay assumes an exact model of the dynamics.
3. **Regression to the mean.** A legacy before/after feedback rule labelled 67–73% of *no-op* placebo mutations (Static arm) as "successes" versus 77–81% for A5's own updates (Table A4, v0.6 conditions), demonstrating a bias that motivated the counterfactual rule. The placebo comparison was computed on the earlier v0.6 data, not on v0.7.
4. **External validity.** Synthetic random geometric graphs, 40/80 nodes, particular failure rates, honest nodes, logically centralised control, one gate configuration.
5. **Statistical.** 60 seeds per cell give limited power for effects below about 0.02; Holm correction applied to evaluable tests only (11 of 12 primary, 38 of 42 secondary); conditions are not pooled so no overall significance is claimed; CIs for some practically-equivalent verdicts have lower bounds close to the margin.
6. **Missing data.** A1 in S3 at M = 3 excluded (2,640 of 2,700 runs); the excluded arm-condition cannot be compared.
7. **Provenance.** The held-out evaluation ran on a clean tracked tree at commit 7a81352 (recorded in `results/v0.7/eval/run_info.json`). The tag `v0.7` points to the earlier protocol commit f874ad7; the two are not the same and the evaluated code is identified by the commit, not the tag.

## 9 Future Work

(a) Component ablation of A5 (E-only, +C, +V, fixed vs adaptive Θ, feedback on/off) to test whether any single signal helps; (b) hybrid policies that front-load and then adapt; (c) per-seed matched mutation counts; (d) additional scenario families, real failure traces and larger scale; (e) genuinely distributed controllers and delayed information; (f) implementing and testing load, traffic and trust signals, and a meaningful "resilience cost" for M; (g) power analysis for smaller effects.

## 10 Conclusion

Under a frozen, pre-registered evaluation of eight timing policies in six simulated conditions, full TCCE-R did not outperform a simple reactive baseline, and a front-loaded schedule had lower consensus error than TCCE-R in every condition. The finding is a bounded negative result on the full resilience-aware design, not a refutation of the individual signals, and it comes with the protocol and diagnostics required to audit and extend it.
