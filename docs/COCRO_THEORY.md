# COCRO: theoretical properties of what is implemented (assumption-dependent; no unconditional claims)

Scope: COCRO-final (`cocro_m`, `docs/R3_FREEZE.md`), its exploratory variants, and the C1 ledger used as a baseline. Every proposition below is about the **code as committed**, with the code location and the test that checks it numerically (`tests/test_cocro_theory.py`). **Established** = a standard result applied here; **new** = proved here under stated assumptions. Nothing here proves that COCRO lowers the consensus error: that is an empirical question, answered (or not) by the held-out evaluation.

## 0. Setting and assumptions
n nodes; epochs k = 0..K-1 (K = 80); T consensus rounds per epoch; setup delay d (a new link is usable from epoch k+1+d); cap M; strict configured-edge budget B; one decision per epoch after observing the link states.
* **A1** Centralised controller observing the up/down state of all potential links (and the topology), as in the existing controllers.
* **A2** Consensus: in every round, Metropolis weights on the links that survive the round (links fail independently per round with probability p_tr); W_t is symmetric and doubly stochastic; x(0) is redrawn every epoch with i.i.d. entries of common variance sigma^2.
* **A3** The controller's failure model (CFPE) is a model, not the truth: statements about estimators are statements about the model.
* **Metric** E_k = 1 - min(1, ln e_k(T) / ln eps) with e_k(T) = ||x(T) - xbar 1|| / ||x(0) - xbar 1||.

## 1. Feasibility (budget, cap, cooldown, gate) — new, by construction
**Proposition 1.** In every run of every COCRO variant: (a) the number of mutations is at most M; (b) at most one mutation per epoch and consecutive mutations are at least 2 epochs apart; (c) the number of configured links never exceeds B (rewires preserve it; pure additions are allowed only below B, and the gate also rejects an addition that would exceed B); (d) no link is touched twice within `edge_cooldown` epochs; (e) every applied mutation passed `SafetyGate.check` at its decision epoch.
*Proof.* `CocroArm.decide` returns None if `n_mutations >= M` or `k - last_mutation_epoch < 2` and increments the counter only when it returns an action (a, b). Candidates come from `candidate_pairs`, which proposes additions only if the configured count is below B and otherwise pairs one removal with one addition (so the count is preserved); the tabu mask removes recently touched links from both lists, and the gate re-checks tabu, budget and degree (c, d). Every candidate is filtered by `gate.check` before it is evaluated, and only gate-accepted candidates can be returned (e). The engine applies at most one mutation per epoch. ∎

**Proposition 2 (no controller-induced disconnection at decision time).** Let c(.) be the number of connected components of the live graph. For every mutation accepted by the gate at epoch k, c(G_after) <= c(G_before) and c(G_interim) <= c(G_before), where G_interim has the removed link absent and the added link not yet usable (break-before-make); if c(G_before) = 1 then lambda_2(G_after) and lambda_2(G_interim) are at least min(lambda_2^min, lambda_2(G_before)).
*Proof.* These are rules 1, 2 and 6 of `SafetyGate.check` (`tccer/core/safety.py`). ∎
*Limit.* Exogenous failures during the setup window can still disconnect the network; the proposition says nothing about them.

## 2. Consensus error
**Proposition 3.** Fix an epoch topology. (i) *Exact mean-square contraction (established, linear algebra).* If W is symmetric, doubly stochastic and constant, and x(0) has i.i.d. entries of variance sigma^2, then E||W^T x(0) - xbar 1||^2 = sigma^2 * sum_{i>=2} lambda_i(W)^{2T}, hence E||x(T)-xbar 1||^2 / E||x(0)-xbar 1||^2 = (1/(n-1)) sum_{i>=2} lambda_i^{2T} <= rho^{2T} with rho = max_{i>=2} |lambda_i|. If the graph has c components the ratio is at least (c-1)/(n-1). (ii) *Random link outages (established; Boyd, Ghosh, Prabhakar, Shah 2006).* If the W_t are i.i.d. symmetric doubly stochastic, then E||x(T)-xbar 1||^2 <= lambda_2(E[W_t^2])^T ||x(0)-xbar 1||^2 for every x(0). (iii) *What the surrogate is.* `spectral_E` evaluates the metric's formula at the mean-square ratio of (i) with survival-scaled expected weights. It is **not** a bound on E_k: E_k uses the realised ratio inside a logarithm and is clipped to [0,1] (Jensen gap and clipping), and the expected-weight matrix is an approximation of the random W_t. The measured gap is a surrogate that is 0.04-0.09 above the simulated mean E at mid-range, with the ordering of graphs preserved (`tests/test_cocro_value.py`); the calibration map is the (unused in the final algorithm) correction.
*Proof (i).* x(T) - xbar 1 = W^T c with c = (I - J) x(0), J = 11^T/n, because W preserves the average. Cov(c) = sigma^2 (I - J), so E||W^T c||^2 = sigma^2 tr(W^{2T}(I - J)) = sigma^2 (sum_i lambda_i^{2T} - 1) = sigma^2 sum_{i>=2} lambda_i^{2T} when lambda_1 = 1 is simple (connected); the disconnected case follows because lambda = 1 has multiplicity c. (ii) E[||x_{t+1}-xbar 1||^2 | x_t] = (x_t - xbar 1)^T E[W^2] (x_t - xbar 1) <= lambda_2(E[W^2]) ||x_t - xbar 1||^2 since E[W^2] is symmetric with eigenvector 1 and x_t - xbar 1 is orthogonal to 1; iterate. ∎
*Across epochs.* x(0) is redrawn each epoch, so nothing accumulates across epochs and no joint-connectivity assumption is needed for per-epoch statements. No statement about E_k of a time-varying topology beyond (i)-(ii) is claimed.

## 3. Selection
**Proposition 4 (sample-average selection).** Let G_s(a) be the gain of candidate a in scenario s (scenarios i.i.d. from the model, shared by all candidates) and g(a) = E[G_s(a)]. Then ghat(a) = mean_s G_s(a) is unbiased for g(a); if the maximiser a* of g over the finite candidate set is unique, argmax ghat -> a* almost surely as S -> infinity; and if |G_s(a*) - G_s(a)| <= R for all a, then P(ghat(a) >= ghat(a*)) <= exp(-S Delta_a^2 / (2 R^2)) with Delta_a = g(a*) - g(a), so P(wrong selection) <= sum_{a != a*} exp(-S Delta_a^2/(2 R^2)) (Hoeffding, on the paired differences).
*Limits.* g is the model-expected gain under the **surrogate** error, not the true expected gain; the model may be wrong (A3); the maximum of S-sample estimates is optimistically biased as an estimate of the *value* of the selected candidate (winner's curse), which affects gates and shadow prices, not the correctness of the argmax; `cocro_m` does not cross-fit the selection scenarios (cross-fitting is used only by the exploratory timing variants).

## 4. Complexity (per decision epoch; measured values are reported with the results)
Let P be the number of potential links, K = K_r K_a candidates, S scenarios, H the benefit window, c_eig(n) = O(n^3) the cost of a dense symmetric eigendecomposition.
**Proposition 5.** Candidate generation costs O(n^3 + P) (one eigendecomposition for the Fiedler vector, bridges in O(P)); the gate costs O(K n^3) (each check eigendecomposes); the failure-model update is O(P) plus an EM refit per posterior draw (O(#episodes x iterations)); scenario simulation is O(S (d+H) (P + nnz)); gain evaluation is O(S (d+H) (K+1) c_eig(n)) before caching. Hence the total per decision is O(S (d+H) K n^3) with O(S (d+H) P + n^2) memory, and a `cocro_m` run makes at most M decisions that reach the evaluator. The Laplacian mode replaces the Metropolis spectrum by lambda_2 of the Laplacian: a constant factor, **not** an asymptotic one (one dense eigendecomposition per distinct graph). The rollout variants add, per decision, two rollouts per scenario of about tau/slot base-policy decisions each (tau = remaining epochs), every decision costing one eigendecomposition for the Fiedler vector and a few surrogate evaluations: O(S (tau/slot) n^3). The persistent caches (exact, tested bit-identical) reduce constants only.

## 5. Opportunity-cost timing (exploratory arms only)
**Proposition 6 (established; Bertsekas, rollout).** In the model, with exact action values under the base policy, the one-step rollout policy has value at least that of the base policy. In the finite-horizon i.i.d. one-shot surrogate, the optimal policy is a threshold policy with the price Lambda(t, m) non-increasing in m and non-decreasing in t. Neither statement is claimed for the simulator: the persistent-flow reward does not satisfy the i.i.d. one-shot assumption (verified numerically in `docs/COCRO_TOY_STUDY.md`), the base policy ignores links still being set up, and the development ablation found no measurable benefit of the rollout timing (`docs/PROTOCOL_R3.md` section 12.1). The hurdle family of Fix 2 is a restriction, and "never more eager than the one-step rule" is true by construction.

## 6. The C1 resilience-debt ledger (baseline) is bounded under recoverable disturbances
**Proposition 7.** Consider the ledger as implemented with the forward term off (w_pred = 0). Suppose (i) every link-failure episode lasts at most L_max epochs, and (ii) the consensus error is at most e_ok in at least one epoch of every window of W epochs. Let w_max = w_prop (>= 1, w_prop is the propagation weight), a = w_prop (1 + lam_E), delta = sigma a L_max (sigma the scar fraction). Then at every epoch D_k <= a L_max + delta (1 + gamma (W - 1)) / (1 - gamma).
*Proof.* Each open episode accrues at most w c_e (1 + lam_E E_k) <= a c_e per epoch, and the criticalities of the configured links sum to at most 1 at every epoch, so the accrued mass of all simultaneously open episodes, each at most L_max old, is at most a L_max. A persistent settlement adds sigma times the settled accrual to the scar, at most delta per epoch in total by the same argument; the scar is multiplied by gamma in every epoch with E_k <= e_ok. In the worst case the scar grows by delta for W - 1 epochs and decays once per window, whose fixed point is S* = delta (1 + gamma (W - 1)) / (1 - gamma). Adding the two terms bounds D_k. ∎
*Limit.* Without (i) a link that never repairs makes the accrual grow without bound (by design); the bound is loose and holds only for the stated parameters. COCRO-final does not use the ledger.

## 7. What is not proved
That COCRO lowers E_k relative to any baseline; that the surrogate preserves the ranking of candidates in general; global optimality of any decision; convergence of the failure model under misspecified dynamics (cascades, node failures, regime changes are tested empirically, not proved); any guarantee for the exploratory rollout variants beyond Proposition 6; any distributed or decentralised property (the controller is centralised, A1).
